# ChemEagle paper (arXiv 2507.20230v3) — extracted evaluation methodology

Excerpts from the ChemEagle paper relevant to evaluation criteria, metrics, and benchmark composition. Pulled from the original PDF in this folder via `pypdf`. Full paper retains the figures, equations, and other details; consult the PDF when those matter.

## Benchmark composition (§2.2.1, p. 7)

> "The dataset aggregates sources from a wide spectrum of chemical literature […], combining a newly collected set of **139 multimodal reaction graphics with 1403 reactions** from 64 recent research articles (referred to as the **Research Article subset**) and a prior dataset of **78 sophisticated graphics with 1085 reactions** from 48 ACS publications (referred to as the **OpenChemIE subset**). These 217 graphics originate from 22 high-impact journals across major publishers […] To further test model capabilities on diverse informational content, we additionally incorporated **107 reaction graphics with 495 reactions** from a comprehensive review on N-heterocyclic carbene (NHC) catalysis (referred to as the **Review Article subset**). Unlike standard research articles, these review graphics frequently feature unlabeled product variants, complex molecular catalysts, and layouts requiring complex cross-modal understanding."

> "The final benchmark encompasses **324 reaction graphics containing a total of 2,983 individual reactions**. The dataset exhibits significant structural complexity, with the number of reactions per graphic ranging widely (from 2 to 34) […] Ground-truth data were established through independent annotation by two second-year Ph.D. candidates in Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering, both with extensive experience preparing reaction schemes in ChemDraw, prior training in chemistry-focused machine learning, data extraction, and strong familiarity with SMILES representations. Discrepancies were resolved through a consensus-based cross-validation process."

Graphical modalities covered:
- (i) text-based tables with different R-group sets (IUPAC names, abbreviations, chemical formulas, mixed with substructural fragments);
- (ii) text-based tables with only alternative conditions;
- (iii) variant structure-based tables with fully drawn product or catalyst molecules, or partially drawn R-group substructures, supplemented by related footnotes, text descriptions or additional R-group formulas.

## Evaluation criteria (§2.2.2, p. 7)

> "The task is to extract a set of structured reactions, where each reaction consists of reactants and products (represented as SMILES) and detailed reaction conditions (including reagents, solvents, temperature, time, yield, and catalyst molecules as SMILES). We used **two main evaluation criteria**:
>
> - **soft-match**, which requires all reactant and product SMILES to exactly match the ground truth,
> - **hard-match**, which further requires the SMILES in the reaction conditions to match as well (see Fig. S3).
>
> **Precision, recall, and F1 scores were calculated under both criteria**."

Match unit: **per reaction**, binary. A predicted reaction "counts" iff *every* reactant and product SMILES it contains matches exactly to the gold (soft) plus *every* condition SMILES (hard). No partial credit on partially-correct reactions.

> "In addition, we report the **Graph Edit Distance (GED)** to explicitly measure how many atom and bond modifications are required to correct the predicted chemical structures (i.e., the structural repair cost). GED measures the minimum number of graph operations (i.e., node or edge insertions, deletions, and substitutions) required to transform the predicted molecular graphs into the ground truth. To efficiently compute the GED, we derive it mathematically using the **Maximum Common Substructure (MCS)** between molecules. To address the multi-molecular nature of the task, we treat the alignment between the predicted and ground truth molecule sets as a **minimum weight bipartite matching problem**. Specifically, we construct a pairwise distance matrix using the individual GEDs and employ the **Hungarian algorithm** to determine the optimal one-to-one molecular assignment that minimizes the total edit cost. Crucially, to rigorously account for discrepancies in molecule counts, any unmatched entities (e.g., hallucinated or missing molecules) are heavily penalized by **adding their entire topological size** (i.e., the total number of atoms and bonds) to the total distance. The final reported metric is the **average total GED cost per reaction**."

## Soft-vs-hard match illustration (Fig. S3, p. 50)

> "An example comparing prediction and ground truth under different evaluation criteria. While the predicted reactants and products align correctly with the ground truth, mismatches in reaction conditions prevent a hard match. In contrast, the extraction is still considered correct under the soft match criterion, which only requires reactants and products to be correctly identified."

## Reported headline numbers (§2.2.3, p. 8)

> "ChemEAGLE achieves a state-of-the-art **F1 score of 76.27% on the Hard Match metric**, outperforming the best baseline, OpenChemIE (39.13%), by a substantial margin of 37.1%. General MLLMs performed substantially worse, with F1 scores below 20%."

Soft-match F1: **77.97%** (only marginally above hard-match → ChemEagle handles conditions well).

## Prompt / k-shot ablation (§2.2.3, p. 9)

- 0-shot → F1 = 33.58%
- 1-shot → F1 = 62.53%
- 5-shot → F1 = 76.27% (saturation point; adopted as standard)
- 10-shot → F1 = 76.41% (negligible additional gain)
